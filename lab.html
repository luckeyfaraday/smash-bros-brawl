P2 uses comma with arrows, comma in the air, and slash / comma on the ledge. Gamepads use a trigger with the stick; on ledges, X attacks and a trigger rolls. G uses the down special; P2 uses backslash. Mario: tap G to charge, tap again to spray, or K to store. Link: G pulls a bomb; direction + J throws, L throws harder and F drops. Any fighter can pick up a fallen bomb with J. Kirby: after transforming, tap G to change back. Pikachu: G calls Thunder; stand under it for the burst. O uses the side special; P2 uses [. Hold O as Pikachu to charge and release to launch. Gamepads use D-pad left / right. F grabs, including through shields. While holding an opponent, tap J to pummel or tap W/A/S/D to throw. P2 uses ] to grab, slash to pummel and arrows to throw. Gamepad: left bumper, X, then stick. Run + F selects dash grab; reverse direction + F while running selects pivot grab. Captured players can tap buttons or change direction to struggle free. J alone starts a jab. Hold W + J for up tilt, S + J for down tilt, or A/D + J for forward tilt. Diagonals angle the forward kick for Mario, Kirby and Pikachu. Hold Shift to run, then press J for a dash attack. Tap J during each jab to chain. Jump, then J alone for neutral aerial, W + J for up aerial, S + J for down aerial, or A/D + J for forward/back aerial relative to your facing. Drift with A/D; tap S while descending to fast fall. Hold U as Kirby to inhale; after capturing an opponent, tap S / Down to copy or U / J to spit. With a copy, U uses it and K + U discards it (P2: comma + apostrophe; gamepad: trigger + right bumper). You can walk, turn or hop while holding them. Captured fighters tap attack, jump or directions to struggle free. U fires Pikachu’s Thunder Jolt, throws Mario’s fireball or draws Link’s bow; release U to fire the arrow. P2 uses the apostrophe key. Gamepads use the right bumper for these moves. K holds shield. L selects forward smash, W + L up smash and S + L down smash. Hold through the wind-up to charge; release L to strike, or wait for automatic release at full charge. Diagonals angle Mario and Kirby’s forward smash. For Link, tap L again during the first slash to queue the second. Kirby has five air jumps; hold J after his jabs to keep punching. Hold J with Pikachu to repeat his headbutt. All four fighters: I uses your up special; steer toward the stage with A / D. For Pikachu, W/A/S/D aims each burst; change direction before the second burst. P2 uses all four arrows. After an aerial recovery, catch a ledge or land to act again. Gamepad: A jump, X attack, Y smash, B up special, triggers shield.

A reconstruction in progress.

Rolls, spot dodges, air dodges, ledge attacks and ledge rolls now use each fighter’s original animations. Hold K and tap a direction, or tap K in the air. On the ledge, J attacks and K rolls. All sixteen specials are also playable. Try G: charge and spray Mario’s F.L.U.D.D., pull Link’s bomb, transform Kirby into Stone or call Pikachu’s Thunder. With a bomb, direction + J throws, L throws harder and F drops; J picks up a fallen bomb. Its fuse keeps burning. O uses side specials, U neutral specials and I recovers.

What is still unfinished?

Knockdowns, get-up attacks, get-up rolls and floor techs are playable. Movement and hit response need work. Stage collision uses original boundary segments and body bones; airborne bottom locking, contact ordering and wall techs remain provisional or unfinished. Dodges and ledge attacks/rolls use original motion, hitboxes and protection windows; air-dodge steering, landing recovery, ledge selection and common transitions remain reconstructed. Stage materials and the static space backdrop approximate the original rendering. All five aerial directions are playable; input selection, landing transitions and hit response are reconstructed. Link’s down-air rebound and weaker repeat hits are playable; the common response needs tuning. All three smashes are playable with original wind-up, hold and release clips. Charge scaling, common transition timing and the charge glow are reconstructed. Projectile movement, Thunder Jolt’s surface traversal and repeated arcs, bow charge strength and timing transitions are reconstructed; original layered effects, absorption and projectile clashes remain unfinished. Kirby’s capture transitions, struggle escape, holding movement and spit-star travel are reconstructed. Copy abilities use original animations, hats and projectile scripts. Hat attachment, swallow release and ability discard are reconstructed; random copy loss on damage, hat physics, item swallowing and original inhale effects remain unfinished. Grabs, pummels and all four throws are playable for each fighter. Capture attachment, escape, Clawshot extension and common throw response are reconstructed. Thrown poses use the original shared animations with approximate body retargeting. All four side specials are playable with original animations and hit scripts. Cape reflection size and multipliers, air stall, boomerang travel and wind pull, and Skull Bash charge and movement are reconstructed. Down specials use original models, motion and attack scripts. F.L.U.D.D. charge and pressure, bomb fuse and travel, Stone armor and transitions, and Thunder travel are reconstructed. Bomb explosions use the original 5% hitbox with approximate effects. Tilts and dash attacks use original animations, hitboxes and timing; input selection and movement transitions are reconstructed. Tripping, despite its source chances on down tilts, is not implemented yet. Kirby uses his original Final Cutter animation path, hit scripts and landing-wave model; steering, phase transitions and projectile movement are reconstructed. The held blade and wave shading are approximated. Cape reflection is playable for fireballs, arrows, Thunder Jolts and Gale Boomerangs. Absorption remains unfinished. Pikachu uses original startup/end motion and separate 3%/2% burst hitboxes; speed, burst duration, turn threshold, landing and cancel rules are reconstructed. Quick Attack cancelling and the original electric effects remain unfinished. Mario and Link’s recovery steering, falling and landing rules are provisional; Link’s vertical lift is approximated. Mario uses the original rising motion but one undecoded script event is omitted. Kirby’s rapid-jab repetition and floating transitions, Pikachu’s jab repetition is provisional. Pikachu’s electric effect is approximated. Link’s passive shield blocks projectiles while standing or crouching; its coverage remains approximate. CPU behavior, shield rules and hit response are provisional.
